2-Speed Swerve Drive

Standard swerve drives force a compromise between high top speed for cycling and high torque for defensive play or precise slow movement. This module solves it with a pneumatically shifted 2-speed gearbox: 7.66 ft/s in low gear, 18.03 ft/s in high.

Skills: Mechanical Design · Fusion 360 CAD · CNC & 3D Printing · Python Simulation

Back to all projects

Why Shift?

A swerve drivetrain geared for top speed gives up torque for pushing matches and fine positioning; geared for torque, it gives up cycle time. Adding the ability to shift between two gears removes the compromise: a low gear for acceleration and low-speed torque, and a high gear for fast cycling across the field.

Features

  • Pneumatically actuated ball-shifter for shifting between the two gears mid-match.
  • Low-profile module - easy integration with over-the-bumper intakes and increased protection behind bumpers.
  • 5 in pneumatic wheel for terrain handling and longer-lasting tires, with alternative gearing worked out for 125 mm (~5 in) wheel configurations.
  • Custom Python simulations used to select gearing and predict drivetrain performance.

Version 1

The first revision set out to improve on the 2021–2022 season designs with three goals: lower the drivetrain's center of gravity, open up space above the bumpers, and protect the modules from damage. It hit its free-speed targets of 7.66 ft/s in low gear and 18.03 ft/s in high gear.

Version 2

The second revision kept the inverted-style module for its protective benefits and reworked the mechanisms around it:

  • Azimuth drive: upgraded from a pulley system to gears, reducing manufacturing complexity.
  • Shifter output: chain drive replaced with gears for increased efficiency, decreased weight, and no tensioner to maintain.
  • Motor placement: motors relocated from the module edges to the center, so a motor can be removed without overhead or rear clearance, and raised from the main plate to the top plate for better protection.
  • Wheel placement: moved further inward to optimize space and reduce weight.

Free speeds are unchanged from V1 - 7.66 ft/s low, 18.03 ft/s high - with the improvements going to reliability, weight, and serviceability.
