PCB Axial-Flux Motor Drone

A working drone built out of a single 75 × 75 mm PCB: the axial-flux motor stators are etched into the board as copper traces, and every part of the aircraft - except the rotors and battery - lives on that one board.

Skills: PCB Design · FPGA / HDL · Electromagnetic Simulation · Python Tooling

Back to all projects

The Idea

Can you make a motor out of PCB traces? RF antennas are routinely fabricated as copper traces, which raised the question of what else the same process could build: if traces can form coils, coils can form electromagnets, and electromagnets can form motors. Researching the concept showed that PCB motors have been built before - but I couldn't find anyone who had taken the next step of integrating the motors directly into a single-board drone, with the stators and all of the aircraft's electronics on one PCB. That gap became the project.

Objectives & Features

  • Create a working drone out of a PCB - all parts on a single 75 × 75 mm board, except the rotors and battery.
  • Motor stators in copper - axial-flux stator windings etched directly into the board's layers.
  • FPGA motor control - fast, parallel control of all four motors from fabric, chosen both for performance and as a learning opportunity.
  • Flight control in HDL - PID plus feed-forward flight control implemented in the FPGA, along with the gate-driver control for the motor phases.
  • Integrated sensing and comms - onboard IMU for position feedback and integrated radio communication, connected over SPI.

Design Workflow

The stator geometry is too regular and too dense to route by hand, so I wrote a custom Python plugin for KiCad that generates the winding patterns programmatically. Electromagnetic performance is validated in ANSYS before fabrication, and the mechanical side - rotor and fixturing - is designed in Fusion 360 and 3D printed.

Prototyping

The board is a 6-layer design, and 6-layer PCBs are expensive to iterate. That constraint shapes the whole workflow: each revision has to earn its fabrication run through simulation and design review first, rather than debugging by respin.
