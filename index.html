About

Electrical Engineering Student - PCB, FPGA & Robotics

I'm an electrical engineering student at the University of Tennessee, Knoxville. My projects range from PCB-level design - including a drone whose motors are built directly into the circuit board as copper-trace stators - to competition robotics mechanisms designed in CAD and manufactured on CNC and additive equipment.

My favorite projects are ones that fit complex systems into small packaging: making a PCB drone that fits motors, control, and communication into as small a area as posible, or the packaging a shifting swereve drive.

write-ups of each project are below. Reach me at rmcfarl3@vols.utk.edu, or find my CAD, code, and PCBs on GrabCAD and GitHub.

Featured Projects

PCB Design · FPGA

PCB Axial-Flux Motor Drone

A working drone built out of a single 75 × 75 mm PCB: the motor stators are etched into the copper traces, and an FPGA drives all four motors in parallel with VHDL-coded flight control and sensor fusion. Everything is on the one board except the rotors and battery.

View project

Robotics · Mechanical Design

2-Speed Swerve Drive

A shifting swerve drive that resolves the speed-versus-torque tradeoff: a pneumatic ball-shifter switches between a 7.66 ft/s low gear and an 18.03 ft/s high gear, in a low-profile module refined across two design revisions.

View project

Competition Robotics

FRC 2024 Robot

A competition robot built on the shifting swerve drivetrain, with a 2-sided intake and a 2-stage cascading elevator - designed in Fusion 360 and manufactured on CNC mill, lathe, water jet, fiber laser, and SLS/FDM 3D printers.
